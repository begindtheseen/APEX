# REDLINE — research and maintenance map

REDLINE is a mechanism-first social-media curriculum for a true beginner who
wants to become a professional creator, strategist, account manager, and agency
operator. It does not promise guaranteed virality. It teaches a repeatable way
to increase viral probability, identify a failed stage, and run a controlled
next test.

## Curriculum standard

The course is organized around five forms of evidence:

1. First-party platform explanations for ranking, product features, analytics,
   ads, and measurement.
2. Caleb Ralston's *How to Build Your Brand Workbook* (59 pages, supplied by
   the project owner) as a major brand and operating-system spine.
3. U.S. Federal Trade Commission guidance for endorsements, testimonials, and
   material-connection disclosures.
4. Durable marketing, experimentation, production, and client-service
   principles expressed as mechanisms rather than undocumented “hacks.”
5. Field reps and capstone artifacts. Completion without executed work is not
   treated as professional readiness.

Interface labels, feature access, eligibility thresholds, ad products,
dimensions, ranking weights, analytics names, attribution defaults, policy
details, and market availability are volatile. Lessons mark these **[VERIFY
LIVE]** and teach the learner to confirm them inside current official help,
policy, professional-dashboard, or ads-manager surfaces before acting.

## Caleb Ralston source spine

The supplied workbook was extracted and visually reviewed in full. REDLINE
adapts and expands these frameworks rather than reproducing the workbook:

- Brand Journey: desired outcome → what you must be known for → repeated
  actions that prove it → skills you must learn.
- Brand Story: Catalyst → Core Truth → Proof.
- Waterfall Distribution: pillar content → mined micro-content → native format
  adaptation → strategic distribution and iteration.
- Filming Environment: preparation, camera/audio checks, talent trust,
  follow-up questions, capture, backup, and post-shoot handling.
- Storytelling: hook → problem/stakes → journey → lesson → action, applied to
  origin, failure, success, customer, and industry stories.
- Content Hackathon: protected experimentation, presentation, evaluation,
  live testing, and learning.
- Team systems: results-based job descriptions, structured hiring funnel,
  employee/contractor/agency decision, 30/60/90 onboarding, effective 1:1s,
  and maker/manager schedules.

## Virality operating system

The curriculum uses this diagnostic model:

**qualified demand × eligibility × packaging/selection × opening hold ×
sustained consumption × satisfaction × social spread × audience expansion ×
capture × iteration speed**

This is not a platform ranking equation. It is an operator model. A low stage
constrains the result, so the learner finds the weakest plausible stage and
changes one meaningful variable. It explicitly rejects undocumented fixed test
pool sizes, universal retention benchmarks, guaranteed posting times,
“shadowban” as a first diagnosis, fake engagement, copied trends without fit,
and guaranteed viral outcomes.

## Coverage and graduation

The data file contains 18 modules, more than 100 lessons, four flashcards and
two quiz questions per lesson, adaptive drill banks, mechanism diagrams, and 20
field-rep counters. The capstone requires a real self-owned account, consenting
organization, or clearly labeled practice brand; a six-week publishing record;
audience and brand strategy; a 30-piece content system; controlled experiments;
analytics and client reporting; funnel and paid-media plans; rights/risk
controls; a proposal; and a candid retrospective with failures and limits.
