::: context recruiter-screen The first call
A recruiter screen is usually a 20 to 30 minute call with a recruiter, not an engineer. They check the basics: your background, why this role, where you can work, when you could start and what pay you expect.

It is not technical, but it filters hard. Clear, calm answers to "tell me about yourself" and "what are you looking for" get you to the technical interviews.
:::

::: context pay-floor The lowest number you will accept
Your floor is the lowest offer you would say yes to, decided in advance and backed by real postings. Saying it plainly, without "um, maybe around", tells the recruiter you know your market.

Deciding it before the call also protects you from **anchoring**: the well-studied tendency to drift toward whatever number is said first, even when that number is low.
:::

::: context first-offer Why the first number is rarely the last
Most employers expect some negotiation and leave room in the first offer. Asking politely for more, with a reason such as market data or a competing offer, is normal and rarely gets an offer withdrawn.

Pay is not the only lever: sign-on bonus, start date, title, equity and remote terms can all move. Accepting on the spot out of gratitude usually leaves money on the table.
:::

::: context remote-first Not all remote jobs are alike
"Remote" covers very different jobs. **Remote US-only** often means you must live in certain states for tax and legal reasons. **Remote across time zones** may mean early or late meetings. **Remote-first with quarterly onsites** means you travel to meet the team in person several times a year.

Read the details in each posting; they change where you can live, your hours and your travel.
:::
